# Missing Prasang Search Plan — Sanyam

## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the weak and missing prasang (story/incident) areas within the current Master Prasang Bank for the Sanyam presentation. It provides a structured gap analysis, coverage ratings, and targeted search keywords to help systematically mine future NotebookLM transcripts, YouTube videos, and literature for the exact narrative pieces needed to complete the presentation framework.

## 2. Core Thesis

* Sanyam is not suppression. It is re-orientation.  
* Suppression means fighting desires with pressure.  
* Sanyam means redirecting energy with awareness.  
* Modern youth are not only distracted; they are disoriented.  
* Sanyam is the steering wheel that gives direction to freedom.  
* Sabha, Bhajan, Swadhyay, Jagrata, and Guru-bhav are the ecosystem that keeps that steering wheel stable.

## 5. Detailed Search Plan by Angle

### Aahar Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 2  
**Coverage Rating:** Weak  
**What Is Missing:** We have the scientific/metaphorical angle (Autophagy) and a childhood biography story. We desperately need a modern youth application.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `bhojan`, `upvas`, `ekadashi`, `junk food`, `bahar nu`, `fasting`, `diet`, `taste`, `tongue`, `jamvu`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** A story of a college student or young professional who struggled with eating junk food or eating out, but developed strict discipline (e.g., observing Ekadashi perfectly or giving up outside food) despite heavy peer pressure in a dorm or office environment.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** Diet is the most daily, physical manifestation of Sanyam. Without a relatable story, it feels purely theoretical to a youth audience.  
**Priority:** High  

### Sabha Ecosystem Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 3  
**Coverage Rating:** Weak  
**What Is Missing:** We have great analogies (Kerosene Stove) and peer-support stories. We are missing a clear "Before & After" conversion story regarding the Sabha itself.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `sabha miss`, `kusang`, `mitra`, `sunday`, `regularity`, `badlav`, `shibir`, `satsang`, `mandal`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** A youth who was heavily influenced by bad company or totally disoriented, was invited to attend just *one* Sabha or Shibir, and instantly realized the value of the ecosystem, leading them to completely drop their old toxic friends.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** The thesis explicitly states Sabha is the ecosystem that keeps the steering wheel stable. We need narrative proof of its transformative power.  
**Priority:** High  

### Desire Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 4  
**Coverage Rating:** Medium  
**What Is Missing:** We have high-level metaphors (River/Dam) and extreme examples (Vyasji's illusion, lust). We lack a relatable, *everyday* modern desire story.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `shokh`, `gadget`, `movie`, `shopping`, `iccha`, `tyag`, `hobby`, `phone`, `entertainment`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** A story about a youth or young professional actively giving up a culturally normalized, minor desire—such as skipping a highly anticipated movie, not buying the latest iPhone despite peer pressure, or giving up a specific hobby to honor the Guru's wish or save time.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** Youth need to see that Sanyam applies to everyday micro-choices, not just massive life-altering temptations.  
**Priority:** High  

### Reaction / Vani Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 5  
**Coverage Rating:** Medium  
**What Is Missing:** We have extreme historical consequences (Draupadi’s war) and high-level saintly behavior or diplomacy (Raja Jaisinh). We are missing a relatable youth/corporate example of holding one's tongue.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `gusso`, `argument`, `bolvu`, `chup`, `shanti`, `boss`, `parents`, `insult`, `jawab`, `apman`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** An incident where a youth was scolded unfairly by a professor, an office boss, or a parent. Instead of answering back, arguing, or defending their ego, they used Vani Sanyam, stayed quiet, and the situation resolved itself peacefully.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** Reaction control is the most frequent conflict youth face daily. A modern example provides a direct behavioral template.  
**Priority:** High  

### Professional Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 7  
**Coverage Rating:** Medium  
**What Is Missing:** We have stories about general ethics (Unwritten land deal, Baniya's shortcut) and service without glory. We are missing a story specifically about *ethics in the modern/digital workplace*.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `pramanikta`, `honesty`, `job`, `cheating`, `shortcut`, `bribe`, `interview`, `office`, `ethics`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** A youth who refused to take a shortcut, refused to cheat on an assignment, or refused to use unethical means at their corporate job—sacrificing a short-term financial/academic gain to protect their values.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** "Professional Sanyam" must address the modern realities of corporate pressures, AI cheating, and resume faking.  
**Priority:** Medium  

### Ego / Anti-flex Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 8  
**Coverage Rating:** Strong  
**What Is Missing:** We have stories about showing off wealth and dropping intellectual arrogance (Narada Muni). We could use a highly relatable *academic or corporate* intellectual ego story.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `buddhi`, `abhiman`, `smart`, `degree`, `logic`, `tark`, `educated`, `engineer`, `doctor`, `MBA`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** Look for a highly educated modern professional (e.g., an engineer, doctor, or MBA) who thought they were too smart for basic spiritual practices or obedience, but had to drop their intellectual arrogance and surrender to the simple teachings of the Sabha to find actual peace.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** "Anti-flex" isn't just about money; it's about the modern obsession with being "the smartest person in the room."  
**Priority:** Medium  

### Attention Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 9  
**Coverage Rating:** Strong  
**What Is Missing:** We have excellent digital/tech examples and ancient metaphors. We are missing a real-world *interpersonal* or *physical* attention story.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `ekavdhan`, `najar`, `dhyan`, `crowd`, `bheed`, `focus`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** Look for an incident where Swamiji, a sadhu, or a youth maintained absolute laser focus or eye contact in a highly chaotic physical environment without letting their eyes or mind wander.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** Shows that Attention Sanyam isn't just about screens; it's about presence in the physical world.  
**Priority:** Low  

### Time & Focus Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 5  
**Coverage Rating:** Strong  
**What Is Missing:** We have fantastic academic turnarounds. We are missing a *workplace* or *daily routine* time management story.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `samay`, `office`, `job`, `promotion`, `time waste`, `efficiency`, `schedule`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** A young professional who drastically reduced their screen time at work and saw a massive increase in career efficiency/promotion, OR a story about how meticulously Swamiji manages his own immense daily schedule without wasting a single second.  
**Why This Gap Matters:** Bridges the gap between student life (exams) and adult life (career longevity).  
**Priority:** Low  

### Reset Sanyam
**Current Usable Prasangs:** 13  
**Coverage Rating:** Very Strong  
**What Is Missing:** This is the best-covered angle. If anything, we could use a story about bouncing back from a *moral* or *interpersonal* failure.  
**Exact Keywords to Search:** `maafi`, `bhool`, `mistake`, `prayaschit`, `pachu farvu`  
**Suggested Story Profile:** Someone who made a severe behavioral mistake, admitted it transparently to the Guru or the ecosystem without hiding it, and was instantly forgiven and "reset."  
**Why This Gap Matters:** Proves that Sanyam is a mechanism for redemption, not just optimization.  
**Priority:** Low
